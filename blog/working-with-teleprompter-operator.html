How to Work with a Teleprompter Operator

7 min read

The relationship between speaker and teleprompter operator is a partnership. When it works well, the technology becomes invisible and your message shines. Whether you're presenting at a New York corporate headquarters, a Los Angeles studio, or a convention center in Las Vegas, here's how to build that seamless collaboration.

Before the Event

  • Send scripts early—even drafts help operators prepare for what's ahead
  • Note pronunciation challenges—names, technical terms, foreign words, industry jargon
  • Share your experience level—operators adjust their approach accordingly for first-timers versus seasoned speakers
  • Communicate preferences—font size, scroll style, text position, any special needs

During Rehearsal

  • Speak up about speed—too fast? too slow? say so immediately so adjustments can be made
  • Practice problem sections—identify and rehearse tricky passages before going live
  • Establish signals—how will you communicate during the live event? Hand signals? Eye contact?
  • Build rapport—you're a team working toward the same goal of a flawless presentation

During Performance

  • Trust your operator—they're watching you constantly and anticipating your pace
  • Pause naturally—good operators stop scrolling when you pause for emphasis
  • If lost, stay calm—operators can help you find your place quickly
  • Keep going through mistakes—the audience doesn't have the script, so minor errors go unnoticed

What Great Operators Do

Professional operators anticipate your needs: speeding up through easy sections, slowing for emphasis, stopping for ad-libs, and recovering smoothly from the unexpected. They're reading you as much as you're reading the prompter.
